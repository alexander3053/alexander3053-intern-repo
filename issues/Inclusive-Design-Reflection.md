## Working with Vulnerable Populations

## Research & Learn

**Who are considered vulnerable populations, and what challenges might they face in digital spaces?**

Vulnerable groups, including low-income individuals, seniors, children, and neurodivergent people with ADHD or autism, face major digital hurdles. They struggle with high tech costs, poor website accessibility, and confusing layouts. These barriers cause severe sensory overload and increase the risk of online scams. Ultimately, these challenges lock vulnerable users out of vital online services and opportunities.

**What ethical considerations are important when designing or working with neurodivergent individuals? (Hint: Avoid overwhelming UX, respect sensory needs, ensure clear communication.)**

Ethical design for neurodiversity means creating clean, predictable spaces with clear communication and no overwhelming sensory stimuli. It requires co-designing directly with neurodivergent people, respecting their autonomy, and avoiding manipulative patterns that exploit attention or executive function.

**How can you make interactions and content more accessible for people with ADHD or Autism? (e.g., simple language, predictable navigation, reducing cognitive load)**

To make content accessible for people with ADHD or Autism, design with simplicity and predictability in mind. Use short sentences, plain words and avoid using confusing expressions. Keep menus in the exact same spot on every page and name buttons clearly so navigation is easy to expect. Finally, reduce mental overload by showing one task at a time, using soft colors, and letting users pause any moving or auto-playing videos.

**How can we support neurodivergent team members in a professional setting? (Hint: Be clear in communication, respect different working styles, and allow flexibility.)**

To support neurodivergent team members, provide clear written communication after meetings, allow flexible hours or remote options, and offer a sensory-friendly workspace with quiet zones. Focus on actual work results rather than rigid rules, and directly ask each person about their preferred working style to match tasks to their unique strengths.

## Reflection

**How can you adjust your communication style to be more inclusive of neurodivergent users and teammates?**

I can communicate inclusively by being direct, literal, and clear. Also, share information in writing, send agendas early, and avoid confusing jargon. Additionally, give time to process ideas and offer flexible ways to reply.

**What are some common UX or communication pitfalls that might make Focus Bear less accessible or supportive?**

Focus Bear can become less accessible if its design is too rigid, sensory-heavy, or complicated to set up. Additionally, aggressive notifications and sudden screen takeovers can cause sensory overload.

**What is one practical change you can make in your work to better support vulnerable populations?**

Replacing complex terminology with simple words helps people facing barriers like low literacy, language differences, or high stress navigate systems easily. Also, using plain language in my work communication can make services much more accesible.
