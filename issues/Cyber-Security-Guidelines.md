## Cyber Security

## Research and Learn

**What are common cyber security threats in a remote work environment?**

The most common attacks are those that exploit people's naivety, such as phishing or social engineering. Many people click on malicious emails or text messages, thereby granting outsiders access to valuable information. Other common issues include using easily guessable passwords, keeping the same password for extended periods, or using the same password across multiple platforms.

**What are best practices for keeping your devices and accounts secure?**

Best practices include using secure passwords of at least eight characters that combine various types of characters, as well as using a unique password for every platform where you are registered. Passwords should also be changed periodically. Likewise, I should enable two-factor authentication so that, in the event passwords are leaked, outsiders cannot access my accounts Furthermore, avoid clicking on links or buttons from questionable sources and do not install untrustworthy software.

**Why is it important to lock your computer when away from your desk?**

This is important because, if left unlocked, anyone could view, manipulate, or even steal valuable information stored on the computer, this would compromise not only personal data but also information belonging to other employees or the company itself.

**How should you handle phishing attempts and suspicious links?**

The simplest approach is simply to avoid clicking on suspicious links, not responding to messages from questionable sources, and not opening or downloading files you do not trust. Likewise, always verify the senders of received emails or messages to ensure they are from official channels.

**What makes a strong password, and why should you use a password manager?**

A strong password is long, random, and completely unique to a single account. However, people are generally registered on multiple platforms, and it is practically impossible to memorize every password for every one of them. Therefore, it is better to use a password manager to keep a record of the passwords and the platforms where I am registered.

**Why is two-factor authentication (2FA) important, and when should you enable it?**

This is really important because, in the event that the passwords were to be leaked or guessed by an outsider, they would not be able to access my account due to two-factor authentication. Likewise, it should be enabled whenever a new account is created on any platform to ensure the best possible security and avoid leaving certain vulnerabilities exposed.

## Reflection

**What security measures do you currently follow, and where can you improve?**

My passwords are always very difficult to guess, as they consist of a combination of characters—alternating between numbers, letters, uppercase letters, and special characters. Likewise, I always disregard any emails, links, or messages from unknown individuals or sources of suspicious origin. However, for a couple of accounts, I have ended up reusing the same password, which is not a good practice, so I should change them.

**How can you make secure behaviour a habit rather than an afterthought?**

Simply by putting all the good habits into practice on a daily basis every time you log in to an account or sign up for a new one. Likewise, by checking your password manager.

**What steps will you take to ensure your passwords and accounts are secure?**

To secure passwords and accounts, you should use a reliable password manager to create unique credentials of more than 8 characters, enable multi factor authentication on every platform, avoid reusing passwords, and remain vigilant against potential phishing attempts.

**What would you do if you suspected a security breach or suspicious activity on your account?**

If a security breach is suspected, you should immediately change your passwords to a unique, secure one, close all active sessions across all devices, and enable or verify two-factor authentication. Then, check and review your activity history to ensure there is no suspicious activity.
